    Getting started

    Sign in and find your way around

    Everything lives in one app. Home is where you ask, the menu is where you practise, and your chats are saved so you can pick up where you left off.

    1. Sign in

      Go to app.galenai.io and tap Continue with Google or Continue with email. Use the email your college registered, so your lessons and batch appear automatically.

    2. Start from Home

      Home greets you with Continue (your last topic), Start a guided lesson, and the Ask anything… box at the bottom.

    3. Open the menu

      Tap the three lines at the top left. From here you reach Dashboard, Question Bank, Flashcards and Clinical Cases, or search across cards, MCQs and cases.

    4. Find an old chat

      Tap the chat icon at the top right to see Chats grouped by Today, Yesterday and Previous 7 days. Tap one to continue it, or New Chat to start fresh.

    AI Tutor

    Ask the tutor anything

    The tutor answers medical questions using your college's approved content and standard references, and shows where each answer came from. It can also teach in different styles, which you choose per chat.

    A doubt after classRevising before a testWard round tomorrowA concept you memorised but don't understand

    Ask your first question

    1. Type your doubt

      Tap Ask anything… and write the question the way you would ask a senior. A topic, a competency code or a symptom all work.

    2. Read the answer and its sources

      Tap References under any reply to see exactly which uploaded textbook page or lecture slide it used.

    3. Keep going

      Tap a follow-up chip such as Give me a hint, or type your own reply. Use 👍 👎, flag or copy under a reply to give feedback or save it.

    Tip

    Be specific. "Why does volume stay the same during isovolumetric contraction?" gets a sharper answer than "cardiac cycle".

    Choose how the tutor answers

    1. Tap the pill next to Send

      It shows the current setting, Default at first. Three options open: Default, Tutor Mode and Tutor Profile.

    2. Open Tutor Mode

      Tutor Mode shifts the AI from giving direct answers to guiding your thinking. Pick a sub-mode: Auto, Explain, Socratic, Case-based or Quick Q&A.

    3. Or use your own Tutor Profile

      A Tutor Profile is a tutor you build yourself: you write how it should teach, what it should refer to and how it should structure answers. Once selected, it applies to every message until you switch back.

    AI Tutor · Modes

    How to use each tutor mode

    Each mode teaches differently. Pick one from Default › Tutor Mode, then ask. The pill next to Send shows the mode you are in.

    Guided questions to build your reasoning

    Socratic mode

    The tutor answers your question with a question. It walks you one step at a time until you reach the answer yourself, which is how vivas and ward rounds test you.

    How to use it

    1. Tap the pill › Tutor Mode › Socratic.
    2. Ask a "why" or "how" question.
    3. Answer the tutor's question in your own words, even if unsure.
    4. Stuck? Tap Give me a hint. Done? Tap Show me the answer.

    Best for

    • Mechanisms and physiology
    • Viva and ward-round prep
    • Topics you have read but can't explain

    Try asking

    Step-by-step explanations and clarity checks

    Explain mode

    The tutor breaks a topic into numbered steps in plain language, then ends with a quick check question so you know it landed.

    How to use it

    1. Tap the pill › Tutor Mode › Explain.
    2. Ask "Explain…" with the topic and your level, for example "simply" or "for second year".
    3. Answer the Quick check at the end.
    4. Tap References to see the page it used.

    Best for

    • First read of a new topic
    • Watching a lecture didn't make it click
    • Sequences: cycles, pathways, phases

    Try asking

    Clinical cases with sequential prompts

    Case-based mode

    The tutor builds a short patient scenario on your topic and asks you to decide, one step at a time: what to ask, what to examine, what to order, what to do.

    How to use it

    1. Tap the pill › Tutor Mode › Case-based.
    2. Ask for a case: "Give me a case on chest pain".
    3. Reply to each step with your decision and a reason.
    4. Use Reveal the next step when you want to move on.

    Best for

    • Clinical postings and phase III
    • NEET PG style reasoning
    • Linking theory to a patient

    Try asking

    Short, direct answers with key points

    Quick Q&A mode

    One-line answer first, then three or four bullet points. No long explanation.

    How to use it

    1. Tap the pill › Tutor Mode › Quick Q&A.
    2. Ask a short factual question.
    3. Tap a follow-up chip if you want the "why" behind it.

    Best for

    • Last-minute revision
    • Normal values, drugs of choice, definitions
    • Rapid-fire self testing

    Try asking

    Let the tutor choose the best style

    Auto mode

    Auto keeps Tutor Mode on but lets the tutor decide, message by message, whether to explain, question you or give a case.

    How to use it

    1. Tap the pill › Tutor Mode › Auto.
    2. Ask as you normally would.
    3. If you want a fixed style, switch to that mode at any point in the chat.

    Best for

    • Mixed study sessions
    • When you are not sure which mode fits
    AI Tutor · More

    Build your own tutor, add notes, check sources

    A Tutor Profile is your personal tutor. You write the instructions once: how it should teach, what it should refer to, and how answers should look. Every message then follows them.

    1. Create a profile

      Tap the pill › Tutor Profile › Manage profiles… and add a new profile. Give it a name you will recognise, for example Clinical Reasoning Coach or Memory Builder.

    2. Write its instructions

      Tell it how to teach ("quiz me after every answer", "explain like a senior resident"), what to refer to ("stick to my faculty's lessons and standard references") and the format ("bullet points, end with a mnemonic").

    3. Use it, then refine it

      Pick your profile from the list and ask as usual. If answers aren't quite right, edit the instructions in Manage profiles…. Switch back any time with Default.

    4. Ask about your own notes

      Tap the paperclip in the Ask box and choose a PDF. It appears as a chip, for example Clinical-notes.pdf. Your next question will use it. Tap × to remove it.

    5. Check the sources

      Every reply has a References button. Open it to see the uploaded textbook chapter and page, or the faculty slide, behind the answer.

    Learn

    Start a guided lesson

    A guided lesson teaches one topic from your syllabus step by step: definition, why it matters, the key facts and the management principle, then a quick quiz.

    1. Tap Start a guided lesson

      It sits under Continue on Home.

    2. Pick or type a topic

      Type a topic, a competency or even a symptom, for example "painless bleeding in pregnancy". Or tap a Recently learned or Suggested for you topic and press Start.

    3. Finish with practice

      At the end of the lesson tap Quiz me on this or Make me a flashcard to lock it in.

    Practise

    Practise with the Question Bank

    Exam-style clinical MCQs organised by year, subject, topic and competency, each with step-by-step reasoning after you answer.

    1. Choose a subject

      Menu › Question Bank. Subjects are grouped by year and phase, with your progress and accuracy under each.

    2. Pick a topic and competency

      Inside a topic you see each competency code, how many questions you've done and your accuracy. Tap Start, Resume or Try again.

    3. Answer

      Select one option and tap Submit answer. The counter at the top shows where you are, and Finish ends the set early.

    4. Learn from the explanation

      The correct option and your pick are marked. Read the short reason, or tap See the full explanation. Then Next question.

    Tip

    Got one wrong and still don't get why? Ask the tutor in Socratic mode with the question pasted in. Working it out yourself sticks better than rereading the explanation.

    Revise

    Revise with flashcards

    Flashcards use spaced repetition: how you rate a card decides when you see it next, so you spend time on what you are about to forget.

    1. Open what's due

      Menu › Flashcards. Each subject shows how many cards are due today, or Up to date. Starred cards collects cards you've starred.

    2. Pick a competency

      Each competency shows its cards, how many you've reviewed and your recall. Tap Start.

    3. Recall, then flip

      Say the answer in your head first. Then tap the card or Flip card. Use Due and Not due to filter the deck.

    4. Rate honestly

      Tap Easy, Medium or Hard. Hard cards come back sooner. Tap the star to save a card, or open References to check the source.

    Apply

    Work through a clinical case

    Take a patient from presentation to management in stages: history and examination, investigations, differentials, diagnosis and management. You get a score at the end.

    1. Choose a subject and specialty

      Menu › Clinical Cases. Pick a subject such as General Medicine, then a specialty such as Cardiology.

    2. Pick a case

      New cases are untouched. Completed ones show your best, latest and average score. Open Past Attempts to review.

    3. Read the introduction

      It lists the competencies assessed, the stages and the pass threshold (70% overall). Tap Start Case.

    4. Make your calls at each stage

      Select up to three key positives and three key negatives, then Continue. You can go Back any time before finishing.

    Track

    Read your dashboard

    One screen that tells you how much you've practised, how accurate you are and where to focus next.

    1. Check the three rings

      Questions done today, Accuracy over the past 7 days, and Completion of your competencies overall.

    2. Act on the focus card

      The orange card names your weakest subject. Tap Practice now. Daily diagnosis gives you one new case each day to keep your streak.

    3. Scroll for detail

      See your best subject and focus area, case scores by specialty, and flashcards due today with your 7-day recall.

    Good practice

    Use AI the right way

    GalenAI is built to make you think, not to think for you. These habits get the most out of it.

    Always

    • Try to answer before you ask for the answer.
    • Open References when a fact matters for an exam or a patient.
    • Rate flashcards honestly.
    • Go back to your faculty's lesson when the tutor and your notes disagree, and ask your faculty.

    Never

    • Submit AI text as your own assignment or record.
    • Use the tutor for real patient decisions. It is a study tool.
    • Upload patient-identifying information in a PDF.
    • Skip the Must know section because the tutor summarised it.
    FAQ

    Common questions

    Where do the tutor's answers come from?

    From your college's approved content (the lessons and references your faculty upload) and standard medical references. Tap References under any reply to see the exact source.

    Why don't I see my college's lessons or calendar?

    They appear when your college runs GalenAI and your account is enrolled in a batch. Sign in with the email your college registered. If it still looks empty, ask your college's GalenAI administrator to check your batch.

    Can I switch modes in the middle of a chat?

    Yes. Tap the pill next to Send and pick another mode or Default. The next reply follows the new style.

    Are my chats saved?

    Yes. Open Chats from the icon at the top right. You can delete any chat with the × next to it.

    What does "Completion" mean on the dashboard?

    The share of competencies in your syllabus you have practised, for example 49 of 120.

    Everything you upload or generate is stored in your Content Bank with its status. Open an item to review it, edit it, export it and publish it to the linked class.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Will students see AI content before I approve it?

    No. Generated items stay in your Content Bank as drafts until you publish them, and assessments wait in Ready until you schedule them.

    Why can't I change the date or time of my class?

    Scheduling belongs to the timetable secretary so every calendar stays in sync. You can change topic, type, venue and competencies.

    Which sources does the workspace use?

    Only the files you add to that workspace's Sources. Answers cite the file and page.

    Can I use the slides outside GalenAI?

    Yes. Click Export PPTX in the deck viewer.

    Where do I change my password or notifications?

    Settings, at the foot of the sidebar.

    Step 1

    Overview and institute settings

    1. Set the institute timezone first

      Open Settings. Under Institute timezone choose your zone, for example Asia/Kolkata (UTC+05:30). Every class time, deadline and report uses it. Save.

    2. Use the Overview daily

      Classes today, classes this week, courses configured and students enrolled, the upcoming classes list and quick actions to Courses, Faculty and Students.

    Step 2

    Build your academic structure

    Courses contain years, and years contain batches. Students, lessons, the timetable and analytics filters all depend on this structure, so set it up before anything else.

    1. Add a course

      Open Courses, type the name (for example MBBS) and click Add Course.

    2. Add years

      Inside a course, add each year with its order: 1st Year, 2nd Year, 3rd Year Part I and so on.

    3. Add batches

      Expand a year and add batches (Batch A, Batch B). Students are assigned to batches.

    4. Or import the whole tree

      Click Import Courses and upload a CSV with columns course, year, order, batch. Errors are listed before anything is saved.

    Careful

    Deleting a course removes all its years and batches. A year that still has batches can't be deleted.

    Step 3

    Invite faculty and secretaries

    Staff are invited by email. They set their own name and password when they accept.

    1. Invite one person

      Open Faculty. Enter the email, plus employee ID and department if you have them, then Send Invite.

    2. Track invites

      Open Pending invites to see who hasn't accepted. Resend an expired invite or Revoke one sent by mistake.

    3. Invite in bulk

      Click Import Faculty and upload a CSV with email, employee_id, department. One invite goes to each valid row.

    4. Edit or remove

      Use Edit to change name, employee ID or department. Email stays fixed because it is the sign-in. Remove ends access at once; their classes stay on the timetable marked "instructor removed".

    5. Invite timetable secretaries

      Secretaries works the same way. Secretaries own the timetable.

    Step 4

    Enrol students

    Students are created directly (not invited) and placed in a batch. Their app then shows their batch's calendar and lessons.

    1. Add a student

      Open Students. Enter full name, email, roll number, gender and contact. Pick Course, then Year, then Batch (each unlocks the next). Click Add Student.

    2. Import a batch

      Click Import Students and upload a CSV with full_name, email, roll_number, gender, course, year, batch. The batch must already exist. You get a per-row error report.

    3. Find and manage

      Search by name or roll number and filter by course, year and batch. Remove takes a student off the roster and batch.

    Step 5 · Secretary

    Load and manage the CBME timetable

    The timetable is the one place classes are created. Faculty and students see the result on their calendars, so every session carries its competencies.

    1. Import your timetable file

      Open Timetable › Import from Document. Upload XLSX or CSV. Columns are matched automatically.

    2. Fix rows in the grid

      Each row is checked: course, year, batch, competency codes and faculty ID (shown as a name). Rows that need attention are flagged. Fix them in place, then Add sessions.

    3. Manage sessions

      Filter by topic, faculty, class type, status and date range. Cancelled rows are struck through. Add Manually adds up to 100 rows at once.

    4. Edit a session

      Change any field: topic, type, status, faculty, batches, date, time, venue or competencies. Clashes are checked when you save.

    5. Delete with care

      The confirmation lists linked Content Bank items and assessments, so you can see the impact first.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    A faculty member didn't get the invite.

    Open Faculty › Pending invites and click Resend. Ask them to check spam. Invites expire, so resend if it shows Expired.

    The timetable import says a batch doesn't exist.

    The course, year and batch in the file must match Courses exactly. Add the batch in Courses, or fix the spelling in the grid, then save.

    What happens to classes when I remove a faculty member?

    They stay on the timetable, marked "instructor removed". The secretary can reassign them from Edit Session.

    Who can change class dates and times?

    Only secretaries, from the Timetable. This keeps every calendar consistent.

    Why do times look wrong?

    Check the institute timezone in the Super Admin's Settings. Every date resolves against it.
